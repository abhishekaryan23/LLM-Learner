Your review routine

Five minutes a day in the Review deck is what turns "I read that once" into "I know that." Here's how the deck decides what to show you, how to grade yourself, and how to make it stick as a habit.

By the end you can

  • Explain how a card's next review date is chosen.
  • Grade a card honestly with Again / Hard / Good / Easy.
  • Set up the course as an app with a daily review cue.
Warm-up · guess before you learn

You recalled a card easily yesterday. When should you see it next?

  1. Again today, to be safe
  2. Tomorrow, every day is best
  3. After a longer gap than last time
  4. Never, it's learned
The ideal moment to review is just before you'd forget. After every success that moment moves further away, so the gaps grow.

You flip a card and realise you had no idea. What's the right move?

  1. Tap "Again". It comes back soon, and that's normal.
  2. Tap "Good" anyway to keep your streak
  3. Delete the card
  4. Re-read the whole lesson immediately
Blanking is normal. "Again" brings the card back in about 10 minutes and restarts its climb; the effortful recall after a lapse is valuable practice.

What happens in a review session

Open Review (the badge in the top bar shows how many cards are due). Cards from all your lessons are shuffled together, which is interleaving at work. For each one:

  1. Read the question and answer it in your head, or out loud, or on paper. Commit to an answer before you look.
  2. Tap "Show answer" and compare.
  3. Grade yourself honestly. The grade decides when you'll see the card again.
GradeUse it when…Next review
AgainYou couldn't recall it, or got it wrong.In ~10 minutes, then the ladder restarts
HardYou got there, but it was a real struggle.Same rung, a bit sooner
GoodYou recalled it with some effort. The normal answer.Next rung up
EasyInstant and effortless.Skips a rung

The rungs are about 4 hours, 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 90 and 180 days (the picture stops at 90). The bottom rung is the restart point: after Again a card comes back in about 10 minutes, later in the same session, and your next Good sends it to 1 day. New cards start at 1 day, or at 4 hours if the lesson's quiz or explain-it-back didn't go smoothly. This expanding schedule follows the spacing research: the best gap grows as the memory gets more stable. Cards you know well quickly drift to monthly visits, so your daily load stays small even after 50 lessons.

Explain like I'm 5

Imagine friends you want to stay close to. A new friend you call tomorrow. After a few good chats you call every week, then every month. If you suddenly can't remember their name, you call them soon again! The deck does that calling-schedule for every idea, so you never lose touch.

Grade honestly: the deck only works if you do

Tapping "Good" on a card you blanked on doesn't save time. It schedules the card for when you'll have completely forgotten it. "Again" is not failure. Each lapse is just your brain saying "not yet," and the very next recall after a lapse is one of the most valuable moments in learning.

Tip: nothing due but want more? The Review page has Practise early. It quizzes you on upcoming cards without changing their schedule, so you can't accidentally ruin the spacing.

Make it automatic

Install it like an app

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari): Share → Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu → Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  • Desktop (Chrome / Edge): the install icon at the right of the address bar.

After your first visit the course works offline, so reviews work on a plane too.

Anchor it to something you already do

Habits stick best when tied to an existing cue: "after my morning coffee, I clear my reviews." Keep the bar low: just the due cards. Most days that's 2–5 minutes.

Your progress lives in this browser

Progress and cards are stored only on this device, with no account and no tracking. To move them to another device, use Export / Import on the Review page.

Explain it back

Why do the gaps between reviews keep growing, and what should you do when you blank on a card?

Model answer: Each successful recall makes the memory more stable, so it can safely wait longer before the next review. The best time to review is just before you'd forget. If you blank, tap Again: the card comes back soon and climbs the ladder from the bottom.
Check yourself

You recalled a card, but only after 20 seconds of struggle. Which grade?

  1. Again
  2. Hard
  3. Easy
  4. Skip it
You got it, so not "Again", but the struggle means it isn't stable yet. "Hard" keeps it on the same rung, a little sooner.

Why are cards from different lessons mixed in one session?

  1. To make sessions longer
  2. Because sorting is hard
  3. So you see newer cards first
  4. Interleaving: you practise choosing the right idea, not just repeating one
Mixing topics forces you to work out which idea a card needs, the same skill a real problem asks for. That's interleaving.

What does "Practise early" do to a card's schedule?

  1. Nothing. Its next review date stays the same.
  2. Pushes it a rung higher
  3. Resets it to the bottom
  4. Deletes it
"Practise early" is extra retrieval with no bookkeeping: grades there don't move the card, so its spacing stays intact.

Key takeaways

  • Answer first, then reveal, then grade honestly. "Good" is the normal answer.
  • Gaps grow (≈1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35 days) as memories stabilise; Again restarts the climb.
  • Install the course as an app and tie reviews to a daily cue. It's usually under 5 minutes.
