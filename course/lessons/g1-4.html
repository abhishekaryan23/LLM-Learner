Latent diffusion & guidance

Lesson 4 of Image generation

Plain diffusion works, but it is slow and it ignores your prompt. Two ideas fixed both and made text-to-image practical on a single GPU: run the diffusion in a compressed latent space, and guide every denoising step toward the text.

By the end you can

  • Explain latent diffusion and compute how much the VAE shrinks the work.
  • Describe how a text encoder conditions the denoiser through cross-attention.
  • Compute a classifier-free guidance step and predict the fidelity–diversity trade-off as $w$ grows.

Why diffuse in a latent space?

A diffusion model's denoiser runs once per sampling step, over the whole image. At 512×512 pixels with 3 colour channels that's 786,432 numbers per step, tens of steps per image, and most of the network's effort goes into imperceptible high-frequency detail. Early pixel-space systems coped with cascades: Imagen (Saharia et al., 2022) generated at 64×64 and then ran two super-resolution diffusion models up to 1024×1024.

Rombach et al. (2022, "High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models") split the job in two. First train an autoencoder (G1.1) that compresses images to a much smaller latent and decodes them back almost perfectly. Freeze it. Then train the diffusion model in the latent space. The autoencoder handles perceptual detail; the diffusion model handles content and composition. Released in August 2022 with open weights as Stable Diffusion, it ran on a consumer GPU.

The autoencoder is a VAE with a deliberately tiny KL weight (plus perceptual and adversarial losses, G1.2) that downsamples by a factor $f = 8$ per side. For Stable Diffusion 1.x, a 512×512×3 image becomes a 64×64×4 latent.

Worked example

  1. Pixels: $512 \times 512 \times 3 = 786{,}432$ numbers.
  2. Latent: each side shrinks by $f = 8$, so $64 \times 64$ positions with 4 channels: $64 \times 64 \times 4 = 16{,}384$ numbers.
  3. Reduction: $786{,}432 / 16{,}384 = 48$ times fewer numbers for the denoiser to process at every step.

In latent diffusion, when is the VAE decoder used during generation?

  1. At every denoising step
  2. Before the first step, to make the starting noise
  3. Never; the denoiser outputs pixels directly
  4. Once, at the end, to turn the clean latent into an image
The whole denoising loop runs on latents; the decoder is called a single time. (The encoder is only needed for training and for editing an existing image.)

Following the prompt: cross-attention and guidance

To follow a prompt, the denoiser needs to read it. The prompt goes through a frozen, pretrained text encoder, which returns one vector per token. Stable Diffusion 1.x used CLIP's text encoder (V1.2), whose embeddings were trained to line up with images, so they already capture visual concepts. Imagen showed that a large text-only encoder (T5-XXL) improves how faithfully complex prompts are rendered, and later models combine both: SDXL uses two CLIP text encoders, and Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., 2024) uses two CLIP encoders plus T5-XXL.

The token vectors enter the denoiser through cross-attention layers placed between its convolution blocks. It's the attention from M1.1, with the two inputs coming from different places:

$$\mathrm{CrossAttn}(h, c) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{(hW_Q)(cW_K)^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right)(cW_V)$$
  • $h$: the denoiser's image features, one vector per latent position. They make the queries: "what does this patch of the picture need?"
  • $c$: the text encoder's token vectors. They make the keys and values: "here is what the prompt says".

So each region of the image can attend to the words relevant to it: the sky region to "sunset", the water to "lake". The same mechanism lets other conditions in, such as image embeddings (G1.7). Diffusion transformers (G1.6) go further and put text and image tokens in one joint attention.

Guidance: classifier, then classifier-free

Conditioning alone turned out to be too weak: samples were often only loosely related to the prompt. Guidance pushes each step harder toward the condition $y$.

Classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) uses Bayes' rule on scores: $\nabla_x \log p(x \mid y) = \nabla_x \log p(x) + \nabla_x \log p(y \mid x)$. Train a separate classifier on noisy images, and add its gradient, scaled up by a factor, to the diffusion model's score at every step. It worked (it's how diffusion first beat GANs on ImageNet), but it needs an extra classifier trained on noisy inputs, and only for a fixed set of labels.

Classifier-free guidance (CFG; Ho & Salimans, 2022) removes the classifier. Train one denoiser that sometimes sees the condition and sometimes doesn't: during training, replace the prompt with an empty "null" prompt a fraction of the time (10–20% is typical). Then at every sampling step, run it twice and extrapolate:

$$\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon_u + w\,(\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u)$$
  • $\varepsilon_c = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t, y)$: the noise prediction with the prompt.
  • $\varepsilon_u = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)$: the prediction with the empty prompt.
  • $w$: the guidance scale. $w = 0$ is unconditional, $w = 1$ is the plain conditional model, $w > 1$ pushes past it.

Why it works: the difference $\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u$ is proportional to the gradient of an implicit classifier, $\nabla_x \log p(y \mid x_t)$, so CFG is classifier guidance with a classifier the model learned for free. Two notes: the paper writes the same rule as $(1+w)\varepsilon_c - w\,\varepsilon_u$, so its $w$ is our $w - 1$; and "negative prompts" simply replace the empty prompt in $\varepsilon_u$ with text you want to steer away from. The cost is two network evaluations per step (usually batched together).

Worked example

A 2-D toy with $\varepsilon_u = (0.2, -0.4)$, $\varepsilon_c = (0.5, 0.1)$ and $w = 3$:

  1. Difference: $\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u = (0.3, 0.5)$
  2. Scale it: $3 \times (0.3, 0.5) = (0.9, 1.5)$
  3. $\tilde\varepsilon = (0.2, -0.4) + (0.9, 1.5) = (1.1, 1.1)$, well beyond $\varepsilon_c$ in the direction the prompt pointed.

How is a model trained so that classifier-free guidance is possible?

  1. Two separate models are trained, one with and one without text
  2. A classifier is trained on noisy images alongside it
  3. One model is trained with the prompt randomly replaced by an empty prompt part of the time, so it learns both $\varepsilon_c$ and $\varepsilon_u$
  4. The guidance scale is learned as a parameter
Condition dropout gives a single network that can predict noise with or without the prompt. The guidance scale is a sampling-time knob, not a learned weight.

The fidelity–diversity trade-off

Raising $w$ makes samples match the condition more strongly but also makes them more alike. Ho & Salimans showed the effect on ImageNet: as guidance grows, sample quality scores (Inception Score) keep rising while diversity falls, and FID gets better and then worse. Text-to-image users see the same thing: low guidance gives varied images that drift from the prompt; very high guidance gives literal, samey, over-saturated images. Typical values for Stable Diffusion 1.x were around 5 to 9 (7.5 was a common default); many newer or distilled models use lower values or have guidance baked in, so check each model's recommended setting.

Playground: turn up the guidance

120 samples, deterministic DDIM with 25 steps, same starting noise every time. Pick a target class, then raise $w$.

Watch three things as $w$ climbs: the share of samples on the target moon jumps to 100% by $w = 1$; the spread shrinks as samples crowd toward the end of the moon furthest from the other class; and at large $w$ the average distance to the data creeps back up, the toy version of over-saturated, unnatural images.

Explain like I'm 5

Two tricks. First, instead of painting a giant wall picture dot by dot, you plan it on a postcard. A helper who's great at enlarging turns the finished postcard into the big picture at the end. Planning on a postcard is much quicker.

Second, while planning, you look at your postcard twice: once while someone reads the request ("a lake at sunset") out loud, and once with no request. Whatever the request changed, you change even more. Turn that "even more" up too far and every postcard comes out as the same very orange sunset.

Where it breaks: the real "postcard" is not a small picture you could look at; it's a grid of numbers only the helper (the VAE decoder) knows how to read.

Why this matters for LLMs: the CFG trick of contrasting a conditional and an unconditional prediction and extrapolating has been carried over to language models. Sanchez et al. (2023) applied classifier-free guidance to LLM next-token logits to make outputs stick more closely to the prompt, and contrastive decoding uses a similar "strong minus weak" logit difference. Neither is standard in production chat models as of 2026, but the idea transfers.

In the playground, why does the spread of samples shrink as $w$ grows past 1?

  1. Because fewer sampling steps are used
  2. Guidance extrapolates away from what the unconditional model predicts, pushing samples into the most class-typical region and cutting diversity
  3. Because the noise is smaller at high $w$
  4. Because the target moon gets shorter
$w > 1$ amplifies whatever distinguishes "this class" from "any data", so samples bunch where that difference is largest. Fidelity rises, diversity falls.
Explain it back · in two or three sentences

Explain how a Stable Diffusion-style model turns a prompt into an image, mentioning the VAE, the text encoder and classifier-free guidance.

Model answer: A frozen text encoder turns the prompt into token vectors. Starting from random noise in a VAE's compact latent space, a denoiser removes noise step by step, reading the text through cross-attention; at each step it predicts the noise with and without the prompt and extrapolates, $\varepsilon_u + w(\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u)$, to follow the prompt more closely. At the end the VAE decoder turns the clean latent into the full-size image.
Check yourself

Stable Diffusion's VAE maps 512×512×3 to 64×64×4. By what factor does that cut the numbers the denoiser processes?

  1. 8
  2. 64
  3. 48
  4. 12
$786{,}432 / 16{,}384 = 48$. The spatial grid shrinks 64× ($8 \times 8$), but channels go from 3 to 4, so the total drops 48×.

With classifier-free guidance, what does $w = 1$ give?

  1. Exactly the conditional prediction $\varepsilon_c$
  2. Exactly the unconditional prediction $\varepsilon_u$
  3. The average of the two
  4. Zero noise
$\varepsilon_u + 1 \cdot (\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u) = \varepsilon_c$. Guidance only "pushes past" the condition when $w > 1$.

From V1.2: why was CLIP's text encoder a natural first choice for conditioning an image generator?

  1. It is the largest text model available
  2. It generates images itself
  3. It was trained on captions only, with no images
  4. It was trained contrastively to align text with matching images, so its embeddings already encode visual concepts
CLIP pulls matching image and caption embeddings together. That makes its text features visually grounded, which is what a denoiser needs to read.

What is the main practical drawback of classifier guidance compared with classifier-free guidance?

  1. It cannot improve sample quality
  2. It needs a separate classifier trained on noisy images, for a fixed label set
  3. It requires two diffusion models
  4. It only works in pixel space
The classifier must handle every noise level and only knows its labels. CFG gets an implicit classifier from condition dropout, and works with free-form text.

Key takeaways

  • Latent diffusion: a frozen VAE compresses 8× per side (512×512×3 → 64×64×4, 48× fewer numbers); denoising runs in the latent, decoding happens once.
  • A frozen text encoder (CLIP, later also T5) conditions the denoiser through cross-attention: image queries, text keys and values.
  • CFG: train with condition dropout, sample with $\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon_u + w(\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_u)$; two passes per step.
  • Higher $w$ means better prompt fidelity and less diversity, and eventually over-saturated, off-distribution images.
